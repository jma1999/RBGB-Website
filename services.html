Commissioning

Verify System Design / Confirm Installation / Test

New and Existing Building

End of Project Information Transfer

Commissioning, or Cx, is a quality control process. Although building Mechanical, Electrical and Plumbing systems are made up of standard components, the arrangement of those components and the interaction of controls are unique for each building.

As the commissioning professional, RBGB reviews MEP design for operational and maintenance accessibility, checks that documents are complete, prepares test procedures, observes installation, confirms specified equipment, records issues, and observes system operation using step-by-step procedures.

The commissioning process can be applied to both new and existing buildings. New building commissioning follows defined milestones and schedules. Existing building commissioning often requires dealing with missing drawings, modified systems, calibration issues, repairs, and best-practice solutions. It is especially useful for cost savings, improved operation, and strategic planning.

At handover, owners often receive large amounts of documents that are rarely organized or used. RBGB works to ensure project documents are organized and that staff training is conducted according to owner expectations. Cloud-based commissioning software platforms help streamline this process.

Energy Modeling

Optimize Building Performance

Evaluate Energy Strategies

Reduce Construction Costs

Energy simulation, or modeling, is one of the greatest building design tools ever invented. It is used to meet energy code, gain LEED points, and report tax credits, but it is best used strategically to optimize building performance.

By simulating building components and scenarios, the model identifies where maximum energy efficiencies occur. It analyzes whole-building performance and helps determine the cost effectiveness of energy efficiency measures.

Used early, the model replaces speculation with data. Results are often interesting, surprising, and non-intuitive because each building is different.

Strategic energy modeling can also reduce construction costs. When building elements are modeled together, some code-minimum components may provide no additional energy benefit over lower-cost alternatives, while exceeding code minimums may sometimes reduce first costs.

Energy Audits

Reduce Operating Costs

A Snapshot of Current Building Performance

Plan a Roadmap to Sustainability

An audit compares a building’s actual performance to its expected performance. The process includes discussions with building managers and users, field observations, research, calculations, utility bill analysis, review of maintenance procedures, and a final recommendation report for energy and water efficiency improvements.

The audit reveals cost-saving opportunities and identifies waste. Many measures are no-cost or low-cost and can be implemented quickly. Others require capital or resource investment and are evaluated through first cost, return on investment, benefit, and life-cycle cost filters.

The audit helps owners understand how physical building performance affects budget, property value, indoor environmental quality, and user productivity. In many cases, energy, water, and cost savings can be achieved through occupant training and behavior change.

RBGB provides a facilitated meeting after each audit to present and explain the findings and help the owner move further down the road to sustainable operations.

Sustainable Facility Management

LEED for Existing Buildings O&M

Buildings and their systems are complicated, need continuous care, and are expensive. Without professional facility management practices, operating costs rise, equipment life shortens, indoor environmental quality suffers, and staff move from one emergency to the next.

Good facility operations save money while supporting the mission and values of the organization. RBGB provides professional facility management oversight, including budgeting, strategic planning, performance benchmarking, preventative maintenance, and management of service providers.

Modern facility management enhances occupant productivity and can also include space planning, security, business continuity, purchasing, and pest control.

RBGB develops custom plans for sustainable facility operations based on the client’s needs, culture, and resources. These plans can be adopted by existing staff, new hires, or remotely overseen using cloud-based technologies and practical field support.
